New · 17 October

Light-wallet swap on the public Litecoin testnet

No Litecoin node needed: on 17 October the app's own Litecoin light wallet paid the Litecoin side of an LTC/XMZ swap on the public Litecoin testnet, with no Litecoin node wallet involved. The app reviewed the payment, signed it without sending, sent it only after a separate approval, re-sent the identical transaction, and then saw it confirmed in a block through the light wallet. The XMZ lock, Litecoin redemption and XMZ payout followed, and every app check passed on its first attempt. Afterwards the light wallet held exactly its change: 0.25 tLTC funded, minus the 0.2 tLTC traded, minus a 0.00001 tLTC fee. The whole swap took 94 minutes.

Problem found and fixed: the app only offered light-wallet funding when the wallet was on a local test chain, so on testnet the option never appeared. It now follows the network chosen at startup.

Dropped connections now recover by themselves: if the connection between the two traders drops in the middle of a trade, the apps now reconnect on their own instead of waiting for a restart. They rebuild the trade session from what is saved on disk, the same steps an app restart takes, and retry at growing intervals. They also re-exchange the already signed refund agreement, so the trade carries on. Messages that fail a check still stop the trade, and nothing is retried in that case. If the drop happens during the final settlement messages, the apps repeat only the steps already under way before the drop: the funding proof, and a release the user had already approved for that same payment. No new approval is ever created, and a settlement that had finished stays finished. In tests the connection was cut on each side after both coins were locked, after the funding proof, and after the release, and once more during a refund. The apps reconnected within 33–54 seconds, and all six trades finished with every check passing on its first try.

Litecoin MWEB payments: Litecoin's private MWEB layer stores some transactions in an extended format that the light wallet could not read. So a payment out of an MWEB wallet into a light wallet, a common way to send Litecoin, would have stopped the light wallet from refreshing. The light wallet now reads these transactions while still checking every one against its block. On the public Litecoin testnet, 0.02 tLTC was sent from an MWEB-only wallet to a light wallet: the previous version failed to refresh, and the fixed version showed exactly 0.02 tLTC more.

Fast light-wallet startup: the light wallet used to check every Litecoin block header from the start of the chain each time it started, about 19 minutes on testnet. It now starts from a built-in checkpoint and, on restart, rechecks proof of work only for the most recent headers, while still checking that every saved header links to the one before it. A testnet sync now takes under a second and stores 230 KB instead of 394 MB. Mainnet still needs its own checkpoint.

New · 16 October

Monzero Pool is online

Mine XMZ together: the project now runs a public RandomX mining pool on the same server as the public node. Point XMRig at pool.monzero.org:3333 (CPUs and laptops) or :5555 (servers), with your own XMZ address as the username: xmrig -o pool.monzero.org:3333 -u YOUR_XMZ_ADDRESS -p rig1 -a rx/0. Live statistics are at pool.monzero.org.

How it pays: PPLNS with a 1% fee. Blocks are credited after 60 confirmations, and only if the chain still holds the exact block the pool found. Payouts are on: balances of 1 XMZ or more are paid every hour. The pool's first 14 blocks were credited to the atomic unit before payments were switched on.

Built for this chain: the pool was written for Monzero and checks every share with the node's own proof-of-work code. Tests on a private chain cover rejected shares, found blocks, orphaned blocks, exact reward splits and payments arriving in miners' wallets. A pool also lets Monzero be listed on mining statistics sites, and more independent miners make the network harder to attack.
